## Counterspace Capabilities

**Co-orbital:** systems that are placed into orbit and then maneuver to approach the target to attack it by various means, including destructive and non-destructive

**Direct Ascent:** systems that use ground, air-, or sea-launched missiles with interceptors that are used to kinetically destroy satellites through force of impact, but are not placed into orbit themselves

**Directed Energy:** systems that use focused energy, such as laser, particle, or microwave beams to interfere or destroy space systems

**Electronic Warfare:** systems that use radiofrequency energy to interfere with or jam the communications to or from satellites

**Cyber:** systems that use software and network techniques to compromise, control, interfere, or destroy computer systems

**Space Situational Awareness:** knowledge about the space environment and human space activities that enables both offensive and defense counterspace operations

## Case Study: Russia’s Reported Nuclear ASAT

- In spring 2024, news emerged that the United States believes Russia to be working on a nuclear warhead that could be placed into space and used as an ASAT
- To be clear: there is a satellite on orbit thought to be associated with this program – Cosmos 2553 – but no nuclear warhead is in orbit
- A nuclear detonation in space would have two types of effects
- First is exposure to X-ray, gamma, and ultraviolet photos from the detonation, occurring instantly and severely damaging or completely disabling any satellite within line-of-sight
- Second and more enduring is radiation exposure from trapped ionized particles in the Earth’s magnetic field & amplified radiation in the Van Allen belts; as satellites pass through these trapped particles, they would eventually stop working unless they have been hardened against nuclear attacks
- We know this because both the US and USSR tested nuclear warheads in space in the early years of the Cold War
- This is against the Outer Space Treaty’s article 4, which prohibits placing weapons of mass destruction in orbit; also relevant are the 1963 LTBT and 1978 Environmental Modification Convention

## Case Study: Military RPO Activities

- United States, Russia, and China have all conducted multiple tests of satellite technologies for robotic rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO)
- Done largely to support surveillance and inspection of other space objects in both LEO and GEO, most of which are related to military or intelligence operations
- US’ X-37B and China’s Shenlong spaceplanes have both conducted RPOs with objects that they have released; they have not approached other spacecraft
- Most RPO activities have few public details and a few remain shrouded in secrecy
- None of the programs have strong evidence to link them to co-orbital ASAT testing or deployment
- Uncoordinated close approaches: potential for (inadvertent) escalation
- Not as easy to make hard and fast requirements about
- Different risk assessments by different actors in space
- Very few hard “rules” about what is and isn’t allowed
